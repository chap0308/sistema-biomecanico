Most people think they need to do exercises like this to improve their hip mobility, or this, or like this. Now, the problem with only doing hip mobility drills like that is it's training primarily the action of the femur or the thigh bone rotating within the hip socket or on the hip socket. But in real life with every single step that we've taken when we walk and even run, this action is occurring where the pelvis is rotating over or on the femur because the foot is fixated to the ground and the pelvis is moving on it. Though if you're not training this then you're missing out on a huge component of how hip mobility is expressed when the foot is fixated to the ground and interacting with it. So when we train the mechanics of the hip joint internally rotating and the pelvis turning towards that side and the space closing off, we want to do it through things such as a front foot elevated split squat with an emphasis on slightly turning the pelvis to that side on the way down. Or a kickstand hinge which will get you really deep into that hip socket. And when we train the inverse or hip joint external rotation of the pelvis turning away from that side, a split squat but with the emphasis of turning the pelvis slightly away from the stance leg on the way down. Or even a cable rope lift with the emphasis of turning the pelvis away from that side.
